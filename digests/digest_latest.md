## AI’s bottleneck isn’t compute. It’s geometry.

**Dwarkesh Patel** · 2:17 · Oct 22 · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/o5UZtZuPAeY)

**Bottom line:** The video argues that AI performance is now limited more by the geometry of chip‑to‑chip communication than by raw compute power, and that hybrid‑bonding packaging is the emerging solution to lift that bottleneck.

**AI & private markets:**
- Nvidia’s Blackwell data‑center GPU illustrates the “area‑vs‑perimeter” bottleneck: larger chips give more compute but only double the edge length for inter‑chip links, slowing AI workloads despite higher transistor density [0:45](https://youtu.be/o5UZtZuPAeY?t=45).
- Hybrid bonding is identified as the semiconductor industry’s top packaging investment to create denser, lower‑resistance chip‑to‑chip connections, now moving into volume manufacturing after alignment and defect challenges were solved [2:02](https://youtu.be/o5UZtZuPAeY?t=122).

**Moats, risks & black swans:**
- Risk: Existing 2.5D interposer and solder‑bump approaches may become obsolete if hybrid bonding scales, potentially stranding firms that cannot adopt the new process quickly [1:30](https://youtu.be/o5UZtZuPAeY?t=90).

**Key insights:**
- Geometry limit: Doubling a chip’s width and length quadruples circuit area but only doubles edge length, capping data‑transfer bandwidth regardless of compute gains [0:30](https://youtu.be/o5UZtZuPAeY?t=30).
- Packaging evolution: The industry progressed from side‑by‑side chips → 2.5D interposers → direct chip‑to‑chip bonding, each step increasing connection density [1:00](https://youtu.be/o5UZtZuPAeY?t=60).
- Hybrid bonding mechanism: Align copper interconnects, press and heat the chips so the copper expands and fuses, eliminating bulky solder bumps and achieving much higher I/O density [2:02](https://youtu.be/o5UZtZuPAeY?t=122).
