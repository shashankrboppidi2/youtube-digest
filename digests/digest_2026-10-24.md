## ASML is bringing lithography-grade precision to hybrid bonding.

**SemiAnalysis** · 2:00 · Oct 24 · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/2P7DuasEnkU)

**Bottom line:** ASML is leveraging its sub‑nanometer overlay expertise to create a hybrid‑bonding tool that could deliver far higher placement precision than existing pick‑and‑place systems, potentially enabling finer pitch interconnects for advanced chiplets.

**Moats, risks & black swans:**
- Moat: ASML – its lithography platform provides sub‑nanometer alignment capability that current hybrid‑bonding vendors lack, giving it a precision advantage for future chiplet integration [1:01](https://youtu.be/2P7DuasEnkU?t=61).
- Risk: Implementation challenge – translating front‑end lithography precision into a reliable wafer‑to‑die (or die‑to‑wafer) hybrid‑bonding system may encounter mechanical and throughput hurdles not present in traditional pick‑and‑place tools [1:45](https://youtu.be/2P7DuasEnkU?t=105).

**Key insights:**
- Existing hybrid‑bonding tools (e.g., from Bessemer, ASMP) rely on pick‑and‑place arms that achieve overlay without force but are limited in precision compared to lithography systems [0:30](https://youtu.be/2P7DuasEnkU?t=30).
- ASML’s tool would apply its sub‑nanometer overlay technology, an order‑of‑magnitude improvement in placement accuracy, directly addressing the need for tighter bond pitches as chiplet densities increase [1:01](https://youtu.be/2P7DuasEnkU?t=61).
- The shift from “die‑to‑wafer” to “wafer‑to‑die” handling is a core engineering challenge for ASML’s approach, requiring new system integration beyond its traditional lithography equipment [1:45](https://youtu.be/2P7DuasEnkU?t=105).
